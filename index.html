Where recycling breaks

160,000tonnes of municipal solid waste produced in India every day (CPCB, 2020–21)

Recycling works only when materials arrive clean and separate. Follow one mixed bin through the system:

  1. The kitchen bin

    Food scraps, paper, plastic and batteries are tipped in together.

  2. The collection truck

    Compaction squeezes wet waste into everything else.

  3. The sorting yard

    Soaked paper and greasy plastic are too dirty to sell, so they are rejected.

  4. The dumpsite

    Recyclable material is lost, and so are the resources that went into making it.

Three bins, three jobs

The Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 ask every household to separate waste at source into wet, dry and domestic hazardous streams.

Wet

Green bin: compost it
  • Vegetable and fruit peels
  • Leftover food
  • Tea leaves, egg shells
  • Garden leaves

Dry

Blue bin: recycle it
  • Paper, cardboard
  • Plastic bottles, wrappers
  • Metal cans, glass
  • Clean cloth

Hazardous

Separate container: hand over safely
  • Batteries and bulbs
  • Expired medicines
  • Paint and chemical tins
  • Sanitary waste, wrapped

What changes when we sort

Separating waste at home costs a few minutes and changes what happens to everything after.

🌱

Compost, not dumpsite

Wet waste turned into compost feeds soil and avoids the methane that rotting food releases in landfills.

♻️

Cleaner material, better value

Dry waste that stays clean and separate is easier to sell to recyclers and stays in use for longer.

🛡️

Safer for workers

Batteries, glass and medicines kept apart mean fewer injuries and less toxic exposure for people who handle waste.

Why homes still mix everything

Tap a reason to see what fixes it.

We only have one bin

One bin makes mixing the default. Two or three labelled containers are enough to start.

It all gets mixed in the truck anyway

If collectors tip everything into one compartment, households give up. Segregation works only when collection also keeps the streams separate, so homes and local bodies both have a part to play.

Nobody explained what goes where

Items like chip packets, tetra packs and sanitary waste confuse people. A picture on each lid and a simple chart near the bins cut the guesswork.

It feels like extra work

The first week takes effort. After that it takes seconds per item, and keeping wet waste apart keeps paper and plastic clean enough to recycle.

Start at home in five steps

  1. Set up three containers

    Green for wet, blue for dry and a small box for hazardous items.

  2. Label them clearly

    Put a picture on each lid so everyone in the house sorts the same way.

  3. Rinse and dry recyclables

    A quick rinse stops food residue from spoiling paper and plastic.

  4. Compost or bag the wet waste

    Use a home compost pot, or give it to the collector in its own bag.

  5. Hand over each stream separately

    Tell the collector which bag is which, and take hazardous items to a special collection point.
